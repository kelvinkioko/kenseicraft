How to Identify Antique Furniture

A chest, table or chair can hide a century of history in its joints. Here’s how to date antique furniture and pin down its style and origin.

Furniture is one of the most rewarding categories to identify because so much evidence is built into it. The way a drawer is joined, the wood it’s made from and the hardware on it can date a piece to within a few decades.

Pull out a drawer and turn the piece around — the unfinished backs and undersides tell the truth that polished fronts hide.

Look at the joinery

Joinery is the clearest age clue. Hand-cut dovetails — few in number and slightly uneven — indicate pre-industrial work. Evenly machined dovetails suggest late-19th century or later. Staples, Phillips screws and modern dowels point to 20th-century mass production.

Identify the wood and finish

Oak, walnut, mahogany, rosewood and pine each dominated different periods and regions. Original finishes — shellac, wax, milk paint — age differently to modern polyurethane. Solid timber with visible grain on secondary surfaces beats a veneer over plywood for age and value.

Check the hardware

Handles, hinges, locks and casters evolved over time. Hand-forged, irregular metalwork and hand-cut screws (with off-centre slots) suggest earlier work. Uniform, machine-made brasses are later. Mismatched or newer screws can mean replaced hardware — note it, as it affects value.

Match the style to a period

Silhouette and ornament reveal the era: cabriole legs and claw-and-ball feet, the clean lines of Shaker, the flourishes of Victorian, the geometry of Art Deco, the tapered legs of Mid-Century Modern. Style plus construction usually narrows the date convincingly.

Quick checklist

  1. Inspect the dovetails and joints
  2. Identify the primary and secondary woods
  3. Examine hinges, handles and screws
  4. Match the silhouette to a period style
  5. Check backs and undersides for labels or stamps

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell if furniture is really antique?

Check the joinery and hardware on the backs and undersides. Hand-cut dovetails and hand-forged, irregular metalwork indicate genuine age; staples and Phillips screws do not.

Does refinishing reduce antique furniture value?

Often yes. Collectors prize original finishes and patina; aggressive refinishing can lower value, so identify a piece before altering it.

Can Curio identify furniture without a maker’s label?

Yes. It works from the form, wood and construction, and tells you what a label or stamp would add.
